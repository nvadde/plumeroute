Screen 01 · Trend — location-based

Trend view — start on the map, then ask "is this normal?" for that place

Trend is not one county for everyone. Pick an area of interest on the map — or tap a place, search, or follow the location you were viewing in Live — and the calendar, year-over-year chart, stats, and verdict all rebuild for that location only. The brief asks for a burning-activity calendar over a harmonized MODIS + VIIRS archive for a selected area: VIIRS detects roughly 3× as many hot spots per fire as MODIS, so raw counts would fake a modern "increase." The pipeline normalizes first — then a significance test, computed in code, delivers the verdict for the chosen place. Trend is history only — no live plume, no forecast cone, and no smoke-aware route on this screen; routing is a Live-mode decision and appears only in Live.

Why "harmonized" matters

Same fire, raw counts by sensor — before correction, the archive lies.

MODIS raw
104
VIIRS raw
312
Harmonized
~112

VIIRS sees ~3× more hot spots per fire than MODIS. Without the normalization factor, any year-over-year chart "proves" fires tripled when the satellite changed. This correction is the challenge — and the validity story for the judges.

Is it safe to drive home through Route 9?
Caution. Smoke crosses the Route 9 junction in about 3 hours. Leave within the hour, or take the northern route — 47 minutes, clear the whole way.

Why the green route bends north — the routing rule. In the real build, the router treats the SAM plume and the forecast cone as no-go polygons: every candidate road segment is tested against the plume and against the cone for the hour that segment will be driven, and any segment that falls inside is rejected — it never draws a straight line and hopes. That's why the smoke-aware route leaves Mill Creek heading northwest (the verdict's safer direction), stays north of the plume, the cone, and the AOD core, and reaches Downtown in 47 minutes — hours before smoke is due there at about +5 h. The red dashed line is the usual route: it cuts straight through the plume, so it's flagged "through smoke" and greyed back. And if your start point is inside the plume, the app says so before routing — "You are in the smoke — leave heading northwest" — instead of quietly routing you out of the zone. The start here is outside the plume, and the map chip on the start point says that too.

Color is never the only signal

Every verdict pairs color with a distinct icon (◉ ◎ ○) and the word itself — readable in grayscale, in sunlight, and for color-blind users.

Built for stress, not browsing

Verdict text is 15px minimum, primary actions are 44px+ targets, and the one question that matters — "am I in the smoke's path?" — is answerable in under 3 seconds.

Captions & spoken parity

Every voice answer also renders as text (the demo video rule: judges watch muted). Spoken verdicts lead with the verdict word, then the reason.

How this was designed

Using Meta to design UI/UX: Image for the look, Spark for the build

Two tools, two different jobs. Muse Image sketches what a screen could feel like; Muse Spark produces the design system, real HTML wireframes, and flow diagrams as code. This page is itself a Spark-built artifact — the prompts below are the ones that produce work like it.

Muse Image

Pictures of screens · look & feel
  • Best at: mood and direction — the dark emergency theme, how dense the map should feel, a verdict card's visual weight.
  • Iterate by edit: "make the map full-bleed, move the verdict to a bottom sheet, more contrast on the cone."
  • Watch out: it garbles small text and misdraws arrows and charts. Never use it for flow diagrams, data, or final screens.
  • Submission rule: AI-generated images on the project page must be watermarked / disclosed.

Muse Spark 1.3

The workhorse · systems, wireframes, flows
  • Best at: design tokens, clickable HTML wireframes, screen lists, and Mermaid flow diagrams that render exactly.
  • Ask for artifacts, not advice: "give me the CSS variables," "give me the Mermaid source," "give me the component list."
  • Same model, second job: Spark is also PlumeRoute's runtime agent — the verdict wording in the product itself.
  • Design now, build Saturday: a static mockup with placeholder data is prep; wiring live FIRMS data is the hackathon build.
